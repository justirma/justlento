Kokedama care

Your moss ball is a tiny ecosystem. Here's how to keep it happy after you take it home.

  1. Water by soaking When the ball feels light and the moss looks dry (about once a week), set it in a bowl of room-temperature water for 10 to 15 minutes, until it feels heavy again.
  2. Let it drain Gently squeeze out the extra water and let it drip on a towel or in the sink before putting it back. Never leave it sitting in a puddle.
  3. Bright, indirect light Near a window with filtered light is perfect. Direct afternoon sun will scorch the moss and the plant.
  4. Mist in between A light misting every few days keeps the moss green, especially in dry or heated rooms.
  5. Feed lightly In spring and summer, add a few drops of diluted liquid fertilizer to the soaking water about once a month. Skip it in winter.
  6. Rotate and trim Turn it every so often so the plant grows evenly. Snip yellowing leaves at the base.

Reading the signs

  • Ball feels light, moss browning or crispy: too dry. Give it a longer soak.
  • Mushy, smelly, or moldy: too wet. Let it dry out a bit longer between soaks and give it more air.
  • Droopy leaves: thirsty if the ball is light, overwatered if it's soggy. Check the weight first.
  • Leggy, pale growth: not enough light. Move it somewhere brighter.

Care needs vary by plant, so ask us at the event about the one you make.
